Who we are

About Us

A worldwide Christian movement dedicated to helping people engage with God through the Bible and prayer.

Vision Statement

Children, youths, and adults nurtured to Christian maturity, following Jesus and transforming families and the nation Nigeria

Mission Statement

To reach children, young people and families, nurturing them through Bible engagement and training to become committed Christians of influence.

Statement of Aims

Working with the churches, Scripture Union aims:
(a) To make God's Good News known to children, young people and families; and
(b) To encourage people of all ages to meet God daily through the Bible and prayer, so that they may come to personal faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, grow in Christian maturity, and become both committed church members and servants of a world in need.

FULL HISTORY

From a simple effort to encourage children to read the Bible in London in 1867, Scripture Union has grown into a worldwide Christian movement reaching generations of people with God's Word.

The Beginning of Scripture Union Worldwide

The story of Scripture Union began in Britain in 1867 through the vision of Josiah Spiers, who was inspired by the ministry of Payson Hammond, a young American Christian teacher who had travelled to Britain to conduct Christian meetings for children.

Concerned that many children were not reading the Bible during their holidays, Josiah Spiers began a simple Bible-reading initiative designed to make Scripture engaging and understandable to children.

On 2 June 1867, he started the work at the home of Thomas "Pious" Hughes in London, where children were taught Bible stories about Jesus, hymns and choruses in ways that they could easily understand.

The movement was initially known as the Children's Special Service Mission (CSSM). On 30 March 1868, CSSM was formally adopted as the name of the ministry.

The vision continued to grow, and before the end of 1868, Josiah Spiers left his business to become the first full-time staff member of CSSM, dedicating himself entirely to the work.

The Birth of the Bible Reading Movement

As the ministry developed, the need to encourage children not only to hear Bible stories but also to develop a personal habit of reading Scripture became increasingly important.

Annie Martson proposed the idea of providing children with daily portions of Scripture to read. She developed Bible-reading cards containing lists of Bible passages for children to read throughout the week and discuss on the following Sunday.

On 1 April 1879, the daily Bible-reading notes were officially launched. This development marked an important transition in the ministry's history and contributed to the emergence of the name Children's Scripture Union.

The emphasis on personal and regular Bible reading became one of the defining characteristics of the movement and eventually gave rise to the name Scripture Union.

Expansion of the Ministry

The work continued to expand beyond Bible reading. In 1892, camping ministry began, initially with boys. This created another avenue through which children and young people could experience Christian fellowship, teaching and discipleship.

By the 1940s, the ministry had also developed a strong Schools Ministry, extending its influence into educational institutions and creating opportunities to reach children and young people where they studied.

A major development came during the Old Jordan Conference in 1960, when the decision was made to establish a Pilgrims Ministry to provide a support base for the work. This development further strengthened the identity and structure of the movement as Scripture Union.

Growth into an International Movement

By the beginning of the twentieth century, Scripture Union had expanded beyond Britain through the work of missionaries and Christian workers.

The movement gradually established national expressions in different parts of the world, including Europe, Australia, Asia, Africa and the Americas.

As Scripture Union continued to grow internationally, individual national movements developed their own structures while remaining connected through the wider global fellowship known as Scripture Union International.

Today, Scripture Union has a presence in more than 120 countries, reaching children, young people and adults through ministries in schools, universities, churches and communities.

The movement's international growth has enabled Scripture Union to maintain a common commitment to the Bible, discipleship and the proclamation of the Gospel while allowing each national movement to respond to the needs of its own context.

The Arrival of Scripture Union in Nigeria

The story of Scripture Union in Nigeria can be traced to 1885, when Scripture Union literature was introduced into the country through Rev. Charles Henry Gollmer, a missionary working in the Yoruba Mission Field of the Church Missionary Society (CMS).

From this beginning, the work gradually spread to other parts of Nigeria.

However, the ministry experienced a period of decline during the Second World War in the 1940s. Following the war, the work began to grow again, with expatriate teachers playing a significant role in its development.

As the work expanded, the need for dedicated leadership became increasingly evident. This led to the appointment of Mr. Nigel Sylvester, the first full-time worker for Scripture Union in West Africa. He was based in Ghana.

The Formation of Scripture Union National Council

In mid-1956, Nigel Sylvester visited Nigeria. His visit contributed significantly to the development of a more organised Scripture Union structure in the country.

This development led to the formation of the first Nigerian Scripture Union Committee, comprising nine expatriate teachers together with some Nigerian indigenes.

The committee held its first meeting on 11 May 1957. This meeting led to the establishment of a more formal structure for the work, with Professor Ishaya Audu serving as its chairman.

The following year, in 1958, Mr. John Dean was appointed as the first staff member of Scripture Union Nigeria.

Further Expatriate Staff Appointments

Peter Edwards — 1961
Jane Sutton — 1963
Bill Roberts — 1964

These appointments contributed to the continued development and expansion of the ministry in Nigeria.

The Emergence of Indigenous Leadership

A significant milestone in the history of Scripture Union Nigeria occurred in July 1966, when Mr. Mike Oye was appointed as the first indigenous Travelling Secretary.

Other staff appointments followed, strengthening indigenous participation and leadership within the movement.

In 1966, Scripture Union Nigeria became autonomous, marking an important step toward indigenous administration and independence from foreign sponsorship.

The work experienced particularly significant expansion in the eastern part of Nigeria, largely through the ministry of Bill Roberts, including during the Nigerian Civil War.

The Formal Registration of Scripture Union Nigeria

Following the movement's transition toward autonomy, the National Council was established with full responsibility for administering the affairs of Scripture Union Nigeria.

On 9 October 1967, Scripture Union Nigeria was formally registered as a voluntary, non-profit-making Christian organisation.

This registration provided a formal legal framework for the continued development of the ministry and consolidated the progress that had been made since the movement became autonomous.

The Growth of Scripture Union Nigeria

From its humble beginnings with a small number of staff, members and Areas, Scripture Union Nigeria has grown into a nationwide Christian movement serving children, students, young people, families and adults.

According to the figures provided by Scripture Union Nigeria, the movement has grown to include approximately:

35,000 Children
205,000+ Students
27,000 Youths
5,000 SUCF Members
25,000 Pilgrims
255 Missionaries
10 Regions
41 Areas
260+ Zones
1,900+ Pilgrims Groups

These figures reflect the breadth of Scripture Union Nigeria's ministry and its continuing commitment to reaching people with the Gospel and helping them grow in their relationship with God.
